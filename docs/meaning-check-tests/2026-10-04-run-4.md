# Meaning check test — 2026-10-04, run 4 (text that wasn't translated: a bigger test)

**Run 4.** An independent, larger test of the "can't check" fix for [#38](https://github.com/chrisqtruong/vox2/issues/38) that shipped in 0.4.18 ([report](2026-10-04-untranslated.md)), and of an alternative with two levels that was built in parallel. When Google can't translate something (garbled snip text, gibberish, made-up words) it passes it through unchanged both ways, the ↩ line repeats your text, and the score used to say **100%**.

**Summary:** on held-out text in all 11 languages, **the shipped check works**: gibberish shown as "meaning kept" falls from **96% to 8%**, fully garbled snip text from **48% to 6%**, and it flags **none** of the 6,160 good translations from runs 1–3 and none of the name-heavy sentences. Its weak spot is **text that's only partly garbled**: 35% of half-garbled sentences still show "meaning kept". The two-level alternative brings that to **20%** (and fully garbled to 2%), at the cost of capping about **0.4–0.5%** of good translations at 84. Whether that trade is worth it is a product call; see [the options](#options).

## The two checks

**Shipped (0.4.18):** a stretch of the translation copied word for word from your text (lowercase words count 1, capitalized ½; 6 or more), or 80%+ of your words copied (4+ words) → **can't check**. Otherwise the normal score.

**Two-level alternative** (`untranslated-two-level.js`, not in the app): the share of your words left unchanged in the translation, ignoring numbers, unit symbols (km/h, mph), names and titles (capitalized runs, "Hero of the Soviet Union"), and 1–2 letter words.

| Share left untranslated | Shown |
|---|---|
| 40% or more | **can't check** |
| 20–39% | score capped at **84** (never "meaning kept"), and the hover card lists the words that weren't checked |
| under 20% | unchanged |

## How it was done

1. **Test set** (`make_untranslated.py`, fixed seed). Each of the 120 English sentences from run 3 becomes four untranslatable texts:
   - *garbled all*: every word mangled the way a bad screen capture reads it (rn→m, l→I, dropped or extra letters, cut-off words)
   - *garbled half*: one half of the sentence mangled
   - *made-up*: about 40% of the longer words swapped for invented ones
   - *gibberish*: random letter runs and numbers, like reading an image with no text

   Plus *names*: 30 hand-written sentences full of names and brand words that should **not** be flagged ("Open Bento and Aurora on the Nautilus before lunch").
2. **Translations** (`fetch_untranslated.py`): Google, English → each language → English.
3. **Scores** (`rescore.html`): Vox2's real `matchScore()` on each, with no untranslated check; then each check on the same translation (`reshare.mjs`). So differences come from the checks, not from Google.
4. **False alarms**: both checks on all good translations saved in runs 1–3.
5. **Tuning** (two-level only) used the development split for the thresholds and the first rules. Two rules (titles, units) were added after looking at runs 2–3's held-out good translations, which earlier runs had already used, so its false-alarm figure is slightly optimistic. The held-out half of the untranslatable set wasn't looked at until both checks were fixed. The shipped check wasn't tuned on this set at all.

## Results: development set

No check / shipped / two-level, "meaning kept": gibberish 97% / 7% / 6%, garbled all 50% / 7% / 2%, garbled half 54% / 38% / 22%, made-up 89% / 89% / 83%, names 98% / 98% / 98%.

## What we noticed

1. **The shipped fix holds up** on a bigger, independent set: all 11 languages (the original test had 6), four kinds of untranslatable text, and the same zero false alarms.
2. **Partly garbled text is where the two differ.** The shipped check needs a long copied stretch or 80% copied; a sentence that's half garbage with a few real words in between often has neither, and still scores 85+ because the ↩ line repeats the garbage. The two-level check counts the share instead and catches more of these.
3. **Much of what's left isn't untranslated anymore.** When a garbled word is close to a real one, Google guesses ("Passengcrs" → passengers) and translates it; then the normal score applies, which is right.
4. **Transliteration hides untranslated text from both checks.** Hindi, Urdu, Arabic and Japanese often spell unknown words out phonetically ("hnrh" → एचएनआरएच → "HNRH"), so the translation *looks* translated. Hindi is the weakest language for both (38–40%).
5. **Capitalized garble looks like a name** to both ("Eorfb", "Svfh Pol"), and short gibberish that's mostly capitalized ("Uq Oko wh Eg nzp Pc") slips through.
6. **The two-level check's false alarms** on good translations are mostly Tagalog keeping English phrases ("head to head record", "National Superintendent of the Year") and words spelled the same in both languages (French "message", Spanish "extra"). Those words really weren't translated, so "check the details" is arguably fair, but it's a cost the shipped check doesn't have.

## Options

1. **Keep the shipped check as is.** Zero false alarms; misses about a third of half-garbled snips.
2. **Add the two-level check's 20–39% level on top** (cap at 84 and list the words), keeping the shipped "can't check" rule. Should catch most partly garbled text; costs ~0.5% of good translations dropping to "check the details", mostly in Tagalog.
3. **Replace with the two-level check.** Best catch rates here, but its false-alarm figure is slightly optimistic (see How it was done, step 5).

Option 2 needs a fresh held-out measurement before shipping.

## Gaps

Google answered **786 of 5,610** requests with empty text, in whole batches of 15 (throttling, not its answer to those texts; spread across languages and kinds, worst for Arabic and Hindi). A retry pass one row at a time was too slow (stopped after 30 minutes), so those rows are left out. A few empty answers are real: Google returns nothing for some gibberish in Japanese and Korean, and the app then shows no score at all.
