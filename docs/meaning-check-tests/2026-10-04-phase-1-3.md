# Meaning check test — 2026-10-04: Phase 1.3 (units and "unless")

**What changed.** The [high-stakes baseline](2026-10-04-high-stakes.md) showed the Meaning Check was nearly blind to two kinds of dangerous errors that were plainly visible in the ↩ line: **units** ("1 to 2 days" ↩ "1 to 2 **weeks**", "4,000 mg" ↩ "4,000 **g**") and **conditions** ("If pregnant…" ↩ "**Unless** pregnant…"). Phase 1.3 adds two small rules, in the same local, explainable style as the other checks:

1. **Unit check.** When the same number comes back with a different unit of the same kind, the score is capped and the hover card says *a unit changed: "days" became "weeks"*. Covers time (seconds to years, including "30-day" and "180-calendar-day"), weight (mcg, mg, g, kg), volume (mL, L, teaspoon, tablespoon), temperature (°F, °C), clock times (a.m. ↔ p.m.) and how often ("each week" ↔ "each day", "daily" ↔ "weekly"). A real conversion still counts as the same amount: "2 weeks" = "14 days", 90 °F = 32 °C.
2. **"unless" = "if not".** It now counts in the "not" balance, so "If pregnant, ask a doctor" vs "Unless pregnant, ask a doctor" is a flip, while "unless directed by a doctor" ↩ "if not directed by a doctor" still balances.

**Result on the sealed high-stakes set: critical errors caught 47.8% → 73.4%, false alarms 2.9% → 3.0%.** Translations wrongly shown as "meaning kept" fall from 551 to 281 of 1,056. On all 12,408 general translations from runs 2, 3 and 5, the new rules change **nothing** (no new false alarms).

## General sentences (runs 2, 3, 5)

Re-scored row by row against `main`: **0 of 12,408 rows changed** (FLORES news/travel sentences have 198 number-with-unit rows and no "unless"). So the new rules cost nothing on everyday text.

## What's still missed

- **Role swaps (12.5%)**: "Tell the pharmacist…" ↩ "The pharmacist will tell you…". Who does what to whom is a matter of word order; word rules can't see it without many false alarms. This is the main case for the optional **AI double-check** ([#7](https://github.com/chrisqtruong/vox2/issues/7)).
- **Direction (72%)**, mostly legal (59%): "$50,000 or less" ↩ "or more", "postmarked before midnight" ↩ "after". Several are on the opposites list already but come back reworded.
- **and ↔ or** (part of conditions) isn't checked: too many fair rewordings swap them.
- Remaining units are mostly frequency phrasings not yet covered.

## Honest notes

- The two rules come from the error categories in the baseline report. While designing them, I (Claude) had seen the sealed set's per-type numbers and 15 of its missed examples, so **73.4% is optimistic**. A fresh, larger high-stakes set will give the clean number (next step).
- The goal for high stakes is 95%+. At 73%, the Meaning Check still shouldn't be presented as a safety check for medicine or legal text.
